## 1. Executive research conclusion

**Classification: `VALUABLE_BUT_INCREMENTAL_EXTENSION`** (decision and rationale in §17).

1. **Novelty audit.** Every ingredient used in T007–T009 is established:
   * the T008 sign leak is a classical small-subgroup confinement attack (Lim–Lee 1997);
   * batched DLEQ is in RFC 9497 §2.2;
   * batched proofs for homomorphism images and joint decryption proofs are known;
   * exact cofactor-8 membership via the Tate pairing is Koshelev's method (subsumed by Divide-and-Pair).

   No cryptographic gap survived. What remains open is a quantitative, systems-level question: which proof-stream format to use for a real threshold transcript.
2. **Format family** (exact relations preserved; implemented and tested end to end, including five TCP trustee processes):
   * **V5/V6:** one constant-size batched proof per trustee message for all its linear statements (Enc(0) rerandomizers + pre-weighted shares), with randomized lifts (compressed / affine);
   * **V7/V8:** native subgroup points with an **exact order-8 Tate membership test** (11.9 µs vs 77.4 µs for [q]P per point), per-item / batched linear proofs;
   * **V9:** V8 with compact challenge–response PoK-CSZ (independent multiplications, the GPU-friendly T006 workload).
3. **Security.** Against CGY Appendix I (read from the uploaded paper), Lemmas I.1–I.3 use the ZKP0/DLEQ proofs only through **membership soundness** and **random-oracle simulation** on valid statements. CSZ signs are recovered straight-line through the h̃ trapdoor. So the batched proof needs no per-item witness extraction (§8.3).
   * Cumulative soundness for one replay: ≈ (2Q + B)·2^{−128} + 3Q·2^{−250}, i.e. ≈ 2^{−63} for Q = 2^{64} hash queries.
   * Three proof obligations remain (O1–O3), mainly that commit–reveal realizes CGY's synchronous broadcast.
4. **CGY results** [measured CPU, 4 vCPU; n = 1,024, one paired run; n = 256, 3 runs]:

   | Metric | n = 1,024 | n = 256 |
   |---|---|---|
   | V5 vs V3 | −25% bytes, replay 1.16×, tally 1.36×, proving −25% | replay 1.27×, tally 1.25× |
   | V6 vs V4 | −24% bytes, replay 1.19× | |

   * **Pareto frontier** (bytes, replay): {V9, V5, V6} at every n ≥ 64.
5. **Threshold ElGamal results** [measured CPU, t = 3 of 5, k up to 16,384; second protocol].
   * **Ablation of the batched-DLEQ advantage.** The 1.85–2.2× gain over the literal RFC 9497 structure comes from 128-bit coefficients (1.19×) and one batch-normalized statement hash (1.61×). An RFC 9497-style verifier with those two standard optimizations is **as fast** as ours (0.250 vs 0.259 s). Commitment form alone costs 1.39× (full-width MSM coefficients); a single MSM recovers 1.11× and cross-trustee base merging 1.20×.
   * **Public-input verification** (decoding + exact validation of every point) dominates and narrows the differences to 1.19×.
   * The **joint** proof of the combined decryption halves public verification time and cuts public bytes 3×, for 3 extra trustee rounds.
6. **Selection rule.**
   * **Model.** A count-based cost model (7 operation counts, unit costs fitted on n ≤ 64 only):
     * predicts held-out CGY replays with 13.7% mean error (5.7% at n = 256; it overpredicts by 10–41% at n = 1,024);
     * orders 94–97% of format pairs correctly and picks the measured-best format at 13 of 14 bandwidth points;
     * ranks all threshold-decryption methods correctly at every k.
   * **Result.** Choose **V6** for public verifiers on ≥ 50 Mbit/s, **V5** around 25 Mbit/s, and **V9** at ≤ 10–25 Mbit/s or for archival / on-chain data availability.
7. **Decision.** The work is a solid, honest engineering improvement with strong baselines and a resolved security argument, not an independent paper.
   * **Conclude this line:** adopt V6/V5/V9 and the Tate baseline in the main voting paper.
   * **Pivot** to the one lever that remains: an O(log k) **batched sign-masking (disjunctive) argument** for conditional gates. CSZ is 61–74% of transcript bytes and 77% of verifier MSM terms (§17).
   * The local GPU task should decide whether V9-GPU approaches V6. If it does not, drop GPU acceleration from the research narrative.

### 2.3 Remaining research gap

No **cryptographic** gap survived the audit:

* every primitive used in T007–T009 is established;
* T008's leak is a known attack class;
* the strongest new *construction-level* idea, batched DLEQ, is standardized.

The gap that remains is **systems-level and quantitative**. No published work measures the following for real threshold-MPC proof streams on a cofactor curve:

* how much of the transcript is linear (batchable to constant size) versus disjunctive (not batchable without compressed-Σ machinery);
* what exact membership costs once a pairing-based test is used;
* where the communication–computation Pareto frontier lies across representation (native/lifted, compressed/affine) and proof form (challenge–response, commitment, batched).

T009 therefore invested in a format family that spans this space, and measured it (§11–§14).


## 3. Corrections to earlier security and measurement statements

The T005–T008 reports are left byte-for-byte unchanged; the corrections are recorded here.

### 3.1 The T008 leak is a known attack class

T008 §1–§2 presents the torsion-dependent sign leak as a finding. Mechanically, it is the **small-subgroup confinement attack** (Lim–Lee 1997):

* an honest party applies a secret-dependent operation s·(·) to an attacker-supplied element whose component in a small subgroup survives;
* the output reveals s modulo that subgroup's exponent.

Here s ∈ {±1}, the subgroup is E[8](F_p), and an order-8 tag distinguishes +T from −T.

* **What remains a valid T008 contribution:**
  * the observation that T007's "free lift" bookkeeping re-introduced this classical channel into an MPC masking chain;
  * the regression test;
  * the repair.
* **What is not novel:** it is not a new attack mechanism, and a paper must cite the small-subgroup literature.

### 3.2 Error bound of the aggregate checks

T008 §4 states: *"For a batch containing at most 2^20 terms, the implemented aggregate check has ideal-fresh-uniform-coefficient false acceptance at most 1/q, bounded by 2^{−128} for the selected coefficient width."*

That is not the right bound for the implemented coefficients. All T007–T009 aggregate checks (`sigma::Acc::add`, `batch::Certified::check`) draw

ρ = (u₁ ‖ u₂) with u₁, u₂ independent 64-bit words, i.e. ρ uniform on S = [0, 2^{128}), embedded in Z_q.

**Lemma 3.1 [standard].** Let R₁,…,R_m ∈ G (|G| = q prime, q > 2^{128}) with some R_k ≠ O, and let ρ₁,…,ρ_m be independent and uniform on S. Then

Pr[Σ_j ρ_j R_j = O] ≤ 1/|S| = 2^{−128},

independent of m.

*Proof.* Condition on all ρ_j, j ≠ k. The equation becomes ρ_k R_k = −Σ_{j≠k} ρ_j R_j. Since R_k has order q, at most one residue of ρ_k mod q satisfies it. The elements of S are distinct mod q because 2^{128} < q. ∎

Hence:

* the bound is **2^{−128}, not 1/q** (1/q ≈ 2^{−251} would need coefficients uniform on Z_q);
* the "at most 2^{20} terms" condition is unnecessary;
* for B independent checks the bound is B·2^{−128}, and with an OS-seeded PRG one adds its distinguishing advantage. T008 §12 already states this correctly.

Two further conditions apply:

* **Fiat–Shamir-derived coefficients** (T007 `--v2-coeff fs`, and the coefficients α_k, β_j inside the T009 batched proof): the adversary can grind statements, giving Q_H·2^{−128} in the random-oracle model, where Q_H is the number of hash queries (Theorem 8.2).
* **Membership is a precondition** (Boyd–Pavlovski). The lemma needs every R_j ∈ G; T007 Proposition 2 shows a torsion residual survives with probability 1/2.

## 4. Secure representation framework

### 4.1 Definitions

* **Groups.** E = E(F_p) is the rational group of BabyJubjub (arkworks a = 1 twisted Edwards form, p the BN254 scalar field). Then

  #E = h·q with h = 8 and q prime (251 bits), and E ≅ K × G,

  where:
  * **G** is the prime-order subgroup;
  * **K** = E[8](F_p) is the kernel of [h] on E. It is cyclic of order 8 (checked by `group::torsion8`).
* **Projection.** π(L) = [h]L is a surjective homomorphism E → G with kernel K, because [8](K × G) = 0 × G. Its fibres π^{−1}(P) = L₀ + K have exactly 8 elements.
* **Canonical representative.** c(P) = [h^{−1} mod q]P is the unique fibre element lying in G.
* **Uniform fibre representative.** R(P) = c(P) + U with U ← K uniform. Equivalently, R(P) = L + U for any L ∈ π^{−1}(P), since K is a group.
* **Encodings** used by the formats:

  | Encoding | Wire value | Receiver |
  |---|---|---|
  | lifted (v2–v6) | any L with π(L) = P | uses π(L) |
  | canonical (T008 reference) | c(P) | uses π |
  | randomized (v3–v6) | R(P) | uses π |
  | native (v1, v7–v9) | P | checks P ∈ G exactly |

* **Abstract and wire transcripts.**
  * The *abstract protocol* Π is CGY over G: every message element is in G, and honest computations depend only on group values.
  * The *wire protocol* Π_enc sends encodings. A receiver's semantics are a function of π(·) of every received element.

### 4.2 Representation-security definition

Let A be a static adversary corrupting a set of trustees. It may choose, adaptively within the run, the wire representations of all elements it sends.

> **Definition 4.1 (representation transcript simulatability).** Π_enc is *representation-safe* relative to Π if there is an efficient simulator S such that, for every A, the joint view of A in Π_enc is identically distributed (or computationally indistinguishable) to S(view of A in Π). Here S sees only the abstract (canonical) transcript and A's own messages.

When this holds, every security property of Π against static adversaries — privacy (CGY Theorem 1 class), correctness and verifiability — transfers to Π_enc with the same advantage plus the distinguishing gap. This holds provided the abstract-level proofs are equivalent, which T007 Theorem 4 establishes for the commitment-form proofs.

### 4.3 Conditions and theorem

An implementation is representation-safe if it meets six conditions.

* **(C1) Canonical semantics.** Every honest predicate and every honest output value is a function of canonical values only. This covers acceptance, abort, identity checks, Fiat–Shamir challenges, batch coefficients, commitment hashes and opened values. Raw bytes may enter only the authentication of the sender's own message.
* **(C2) Fresh pads.** Every honest outgoing element is sent as L + U, where:
  * U ← K is uniform and independent across elements, messages and actions;
  * U is independent of all secrets and of all incoming representations;
  * L is any fibre element computed by honest arithmetic.

  Alternatively, an element is sent as a deterministic function of its canonical value (c(P), or the fibre minimum of §4.5).
* **(C3) Separate randomness.** Pad randomness never reuses witness or proof-nonce randomness.
* **(C4) Version separation.** Signatures, transcript headers and Fiat–Shamir domains include the format version, so an encoding cannot be reinterpreted under another format's semantics.
* **(C5) Representation-independent timing.** Honest computation time does not depend on the torsion components of incoming representations. This is a host-side assumption. arkworks' complete Edwards formulas are not data-dependent in the point, but variable-time scalar multiplication depends on the scalar.
* **(C6) Abort semantics.** The decision to abort depends only on canonical values. With randomized and lifted formats a torsion-only change never aborts; with native formats a torsion component always aborts. Either way the behaviour is determined by A's own message.

> **Theorem 4.2 [new as stated; elementary].** Under (C1)–(C6), the lifted-randomized formats (v3, v4, v5, v6) and the native formats (v7, v8, v9) are representation-safe relative to their abstract protocols, *perfectly* for ideal pads.

*Proof sketch.*

1. **Native formats.** Every accepted element is in G and is the abstract element itself, so S is the identity.
2. **Randomized formats, one element.** Consider an honest outgoing element L + U. Its canonical part P = π(L) is determined by the abstract execution. Its torsion part (L − c(P)) + U is uniform on K and independent of L, because U is a one-time pad in the group K, and by (C2) also independent of every other pad. So S outputs c(P) + U' with fresh U'.
3. **A's influence.** By (C1) and (C6), A's choices of incoming representations affect honest behaviour only through canonical values, which S sees in the abstract run, and through accept/abort events that are functions of A's own message.
4. **Many elements.** Repeated, correlated and multi-coordinate elements, and adaptively chosen messages within a static corruption, are covered because the pads are independent per coordinate (C2) and nothing else depends on representations (C1).
5. **Realised pads.** With a ChaCha20 pad stream the statement holds up to the PRG advantage. ∎

**Scope.**

* **Static corruption only.** Adaptive corruption reveals pad state. That state is independent of secrets, so it adds nothing beyond the corrupted party's secrets, but adaptive security of CGY itself is a separate question that this argument does not settle.
* **What follows from the representation argument:** T008's leak is closed; malleable wire bytes do not affect semantics.
* **What still depends on the CGY proof:** privacy, verifiability and correctness of the abstract protocol, and the soundness of the proof systems (T007 Theorem 4; §8 here).
* **Implementation status:**
  * the T008 code meets (C1)–(C4);
  * T009 extends it to the new item types: the `Lin5` commitments are padded from a separately seeded stream, domain `wod/v5/representation-torsion`;
  * T009 adds version-separated signature domains for v5–v9.

### 4.4 Exact membership instead of representations

The native formats replace representations by an exact check. For BabyJubjub, `subgroup::in_subgroup_tate` computes

t₈(T, P) = f_{8,T}(P)^{(p−1)/8}, with f_{8,T} = ℓ_T⁴ · ℓ_{2T}² / (v_{2T}⁴ · v_{4T}),

and accepts iff t₈ = 1.

**Why the test is exact.**

* T is a rational point of order 8, and 8 | p − 1.
* The reduced Tate pairing E[8](F_p) × E/8E → μ₈ is non-degenerate (Frey–Rück). With E/8E ≅ Z/8 cyclic, P ↦ t₈(T, P) has kernel exactly 8E = G.
* The lines are monic in Y or X, hence normalised at O, so no constant survives the exponentiation. Torsion points, the only points where the function can vanish or be undefined, are decided separately: a torsion point is in G iff it is O.

**Evaluation.** The function is evaluated projectively from Edwards coordinates through the Montgomery/Weierstrass map, with no inversion, followed by one exponentiation.

**Tests.** The test checks all 8 torsion classes, 64 random rational points against [q]P, and the character property t₈(P + kT) = t₈(P + T)^k.

**Cost.**

| Test | Cost per point [measured CPU, single thread] |
|---|---:|
| Tate test | 11.9 µs |
| [q]P with wNAF | 77.4 µs |
| Compressed decode | 23.7 µs |
| [8]-clear | 0.76 µs |

Pornin-style halving needs, for Sylow-2 ≅ Z/8, two square roots and three Legendre symbols. A square root in this field costs a Tonelli–Shanks exponentiation plus a 2-adicity-28 loop, so halving is not expected to beat the single-exponentiation pairing test here **[projection]**.

### 4.5 Deterministic coset representatives

* **Fibre minimum.** A deterministic, efficiently computable representative is the minimum of the 8 compressed encodings of L + K, i.e. canonical bytes for the quotient E/K. It costs:
  * for the sender: 7 additions + 8 normalizations (one batched inversion);
  * for a receiver checking canonicity: the same.

  It is simulatable without randomness and makes wire bytes non-malleable, as Ristretto does, but it is about 8× the sender cost of a pad.
* **c(P) = [8^{−1}]P.** Also deterministic but costs a scalar multiplication per element (T008 "canonical" policy).
* **Ristretto-style quotient encoding.** An encoding with a single square root and built-in canonicity would be the attractive option. Ristretto's formulas depend on p ≡ 5 mod 8 (via `SQRT_RATIO_M1`); BabyJubjub has p ≡ 1 mod 2²⁸. A BabyJubjub variant is **[open]**.

The **simplest defensible construction** is the native format with the exact Tate test:

* it has no representation layer at all (Theorem 4.2 is trivial for it);
* its cost over lifts is one exponentiation per received point (§11).

The randomized pad remains the cheapest secure *lifted* format.


## 5. Proof-stream structure and cost analysis

**Dependency structure (per gate, CGY conditional gate; a = 5 trustees).**

* **Mask round.** Trustee i (in chain order 1…a) consumes (X_{i−1}, Y_{i−1}) and publishes:
  * (X_i, Y_i, e_i) with PoK-CSZ;
  * a hash commitment to its rerandomizers.

  This round is **sequential across trustees** and is the critical path of the dataflow schedule.
* **Reveal round.** Once all a commitments are on the board, every trustee publishes its two rerandomizers A_i, B_i (Enc(0)) with ZKP0. These are parallel across trustees.
* **Decryption round.** Once all reveals are in, every trustee publishes w_i = [λ_i s_i]u for u = (Y_a + ΣB_i).a with DLEQ. These are parallel.
* **Release slot.** Commit, then a 1-ciphertext reveal, then decryption. The terminal release accepts only the decryptions O or g.
* **Messages.** The dataflow scheduler batches every enabled action of a trustee into one signed message:
  * 570 messages at n = 64, 810 at n = 1,024;
  * one message carries the masks, reveals and shares of up to thousands of gates.

  This is what makes message-level batching effective.

**Witness and base structure per family.**

## 7. Selected construction

**Research question.** For a threshold protocol whose public transcript is a stream of signed messages, each carrying many proofs, which per-message proof format minimises

T_total = T_prove + T_verify + T_decode + T_communication

on CPU and GPU hardware, subject to exactly preserving the protocol's proof relations? The relations are:

* membership for linear statements;
* knowledge for PoK-CSZ.

**Algorithm (trustee i, one message).**

1. **Produce items.**
   * Masks: commitment-form CSZ (v5, v6, v8) or challenge–response CSZ (v9).
   * Rerandomizer reveals and decryption shares: **without per-item proofs**.
2. **Batch proof.** Build `LinStmt` from the message's reveals (canonical R_k, labels) and shares (u_j from the state, canonical w_j, labels). Prove it with one `Lin` (§8.1) and append it as the last item.
3. **Encode points.**
   * v5/v6: lifts with fresh K-pads (compressed / affine);
   * v8/v9: native canonical points.
4. **Sign** under `wod/msg/v{version}`.

**Verifier (every receiver and the public verifier).**

1. Check the signature and the counter.
2. Decode the points:
   * v5/v6: π = [8] on every point;
   * v8/v9: exact Tate test on every point, reject on failure.
3. Run the state checks, including the reveal commitments `wod/v5/rr-commit`.
4. Verify:
   * v9: CSZ as one batch of independent variable-base multiplications (`MulBackend::run`: CPU or the T006 GPU kernel);
   * all other relations (commitment-form CSZ, `Lin`): one random linear combination with fresh verifier coefficients in [0, 2^{128}), evaluated as **one MSM per message**.
5. If the combination fails, re-check per proof to report the offending item.
6. Apply the items; finish gates with pre-weighted shares.

**Invariants (each with regression tests, §10).**

| Invariant | Statement |
|---|---|
| I1 | Every value used semantically is canonical and in G (π or the exact test) |
| I2 | A message has a `Lin5` iff it has linear statements; it is last and unique |
| I3 | The `Lin` seed binds the session, version, sender, W_i and the ordered labelled canonical statements |
| I4 | Batch coefficients are fresh verifier randomness (outer RLC) or hash outputs over the full statement (inner `Lin`), 128 bits |
| I5 | Format admission is exact: no per-item and batched items mixed; v1-form masks only in v1/v9; signature domains differ per version |
| I6 | Pre-weighted shares against W_i for the fixed decryption set |


## 8. Mathematical security arguments

### 8.1 Statement and proof (formats v5, v6, v8, v9)

Fix a trustee message from sender i with weighted key W = W_i = [σ]g (σ = λ_i s_i, public W).

**Statement.** Its canonical *linear statement*, in item order, is

S = (enc: (ℓ_k, R_k)_k, dec: (ℓ_j, u_j, w_j)_j),

where:

* R_k ∈ G² are the revealed rerandomizers;
* u_j is the first component of the opened ciphertext Y' of slot j (engine state, fixed by earlier messages);
* w_j is the pre-weighted share;
* ℓ are slot/trustee labels.

**Language.** Let x = dlog_g h. Then

L_lin = { S : ∀k R_k.b = [x]R_k.a  ∧  ∀j w_j = [σ]u_j }.

Membership is well defined without a witness (G is cyclic).

**Proof.** π = (T₁, T₂, T₃, T₄; z₁, z₂), with each half present iff its list is non-empty:

1. seed = H₁("wod/v5/lin-seed", ctx, version, i, g, W, S);
2. α_k, β_j = first 128 bits of H₂(seed, "enc"/"dec", index);
3. c = H₃(ctx, seed, T₁..T₄), full width.

**Verification** accepts iff

* [z₁]g = T₁ + c·Σα_k R_k.a;
* [z₁]h = T₂ + c·Σα_k R_k.b;
* [z₂]g = T₃ + c·W;
* [z₂]U* = T₄ + c·D*, where U* = Σβ_j u_j and D* = Σβ_j w_j.

These rows join the message's random linear combination. U* and D* are never formed: their terms go straight into the MSM.

**Honest prover.** κ* = Σα_k ρ_k; T₁ = [a]g, T₂ = [a]h, T₃ = [b]g, T₄ = [b]U*; z₁ = a + cκ*, z₂ = b + cσ.

### 8.2 Theorems

> **Theorem 8.1 (completeness) [standard].** Honest proofs verify.

> **Theorem 8.2 (soundness, ROM) [application of Henry / Davidson et al. / BGR].** For any adversary making at most Q queries to H₁ and H₃,
>
> Pr[accept ∧ S ∉ L_lin] ≤ 2Q·2^{−128} + 2Q/q.
>
> The bound covers adversarially chosen statements, reordering and adaptive grinding of S.

*Proof.* Define the errors ε_k = R_k.b − [x]R_k.a and δ_j = w_j − [σ]u_j, and their combinations ε* = Σα_kε_k and δ* = Σβ_jδ_j.

1. **Enc part.** From the two enc rows, ([z₁]h − T₂) − [x]([z₁]g − T₁) = c·ε*, i.e. −(T₂ − [x]T₁) = c·ε*. The left side is fixed before c, which is a fresh H₃ output.
2. **Dec part.** From the dec rows, with τ₃ = dlog_g T₃ (which forces z₂ = τ₃ + cσ): [τ₃]U* − T₄ = c·δ*, with the left side again fixed before c.
3. **Challenge step.** If ε* ≠ O (resp. δ* ≠ O), each H₃ query succeeds with probability ≤ 1/q.
4. **Coefficient step.** If some ε_k ≠ O but ε* = O: α is a deterministic function of the H₁ output on S, so for each H₁ query Lemma 3.1 bounds Pr[ε* = O] by 2^{−128}. The same holds for δ.
5. A union bound over queries gives the theorem. ∎

> **Theorem 8.3 (zero knowledge, ROM) [standard].** The proof is HVZK and, with H₃ programmed, NIZK. The simulator picks c, z₁, z₂ and solves for T₁..T₄.

**Proof versus knowledge.** π proves *membership* plus knowledge of σ and of the combination κ*, **not** of each ρ_k.

### 8.3 Composition with the CGY gate: resolved against CGY Appendix I

**Source.** Cortier–Gaudry–Yang, *A toolbox for verifiable tally-hiding e-voting systems* (ePrint 2021/491), Appendix I. Read in full from the copy the author uploaded at the start of this research line [R]; eprint.iacr.org itself is behind a browser challenge from this environment.

**What each lemma uses from its proofs.**

| CGY result | Sub-protocol | Uses from the corrupted parties' proofs | Uses for honest proofs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lemma I.1 (Alg. 63 RR → Alg. 64 F_rerand), F_RO, F_SB-hybrid | Every party publishes U_i = Enc(0, r) with a PoK (c_u, a_u) hashed over pk‖X‖U_i‖c_u | "by the **computational soundness** of the PoK, U_j is an encryption of 0". The simulator only *looks for* the RO query that makes π_j valid; it never rewinds and never uses r_j | It replaces one honest U_i by X'/(X·Π_{j≠i}U_j), a valid zero-encryption, and simulates π_i by choosing (d, a) and **programming** F_RO at a fresh point |
| Lemma I.2 (Alg. 65 TD → Alg. 66 F_Dec), F_rerand, F_RO-hybrid | Shares w_j = u^{s_j} with a DLEQ proof | "by the **soundness** of the ZKP there exists s_j with g^{s_j} = h_j and u^{s_j} = w_j", i.e. membership | Picks Y' = (g^r, y·h^r) itself, sets w_i = h_i^r (a true DDH tuple), and simulates the proof by RO programming |
| Lemma I.3 (Alg. 67 conditional gate) | PoK-CSZ | **Soundness** gives (r₁, r₂, s); the sign s is then recovered **straight-line** through the trapdoor τ of h̃ = g^{1/τ} (u_{x,j}·e_j^{−τ}). No rewinding | DDH-based simulation of the last honest masker |

**Conclusion.** None of the three lemmas extracts a witness by rewinding. Lemma I.3 recovers the CSZ sign straight-line through the h̃ trapdoor. The ZKP0 and DLEQ proofs are used **only** through:

* computational (membership) soundness of corrupted parties' proofs;
* zero-knowledge simulation of honest proofs by programming the random oracle at an unqueried point, on **valid** statements.

The batched proof provides both:

* **Soundness.** Theorem 8.2: if any statement in a corrupted message is false, acceptance has probability ≤ 2Q·2^{−128} + 2Q/q.
* **Simulation.** In the composed simulation every honest statement in a message is valid:
  * the modified rerandomizers of Lemma I.1 are zero-encryptions;
  * the simulated shares of Lemma I.2 are true DDH tuples.

  So the simulator programs **one** H₃ point per honest message, with c and z₁, z₂ random and T₁..T₄ solved. The point (seed, T) is unqueried except with probability ≤ Q/q, because T is fresh. The single shared challenge across the enc and dec halves is the standard AND-composition of HVZK protocols.

**Hence the construction requires membership soundness, not individual witness extraction [application].** This removes the [conditional] flag carried by the draft.

**Implementation-level conditions.** These are the differences from Alg. 63/65 that a paper must state.

1. **Broadcast versus commit–reveal.** Lemma I.1 is proved with synchronous broadcast F_SB: corrupted contributions are fixed before honest ones are seen. The implementation instead orders an authenticated board with hash commitments:
   * each Mask item carries `wod/v5/rr-commit` = H(ctx, label, salt, R);
   * reveals are admitted only after **all** commitments of the slot (`reveal before all commitments` check).

   In the ROM this realizes the F_SB ordering. The simulator reads corrupted committed R_j from the RO query table, which needs an *observable* RO, and equivocates the honest commitment by programming H at the fresh 256-bit salt. **Proof obligation O1:** a short lemma that this commit–reveal realizes F_SB for the rerandomizers, i.e. the standard "commitments in an observable and programmable ROM" argument; CGY assume F_SB directly.
2. **Session binding.**
   * CGY hash pk‖X‖U‖c_u (rerandomization) and (g, h)‖Y'‖w‖c_g‖c_u (decryption), so a party with a divergent view of X rejects (Lemma I.1, Case 1).
   * The `Lin` seed binds ctx (setup and sealed board), version, sender, W_i, every slot label and every canonical statement; for shares this includes u_j = Y'.a.
   * It does **not** hash the rerandomized ciphertext X of each slot. Views are common by construction: one signed, counter-ordered board and a deterministic engine. Case 1 therefore never arises unless the board equivocates, and an equivocating board can only withhold, not forge.

   **Recommended hardening (O2):** add the slot ciphertexts (X_a, Y_a) to the seed (2 ciphertexts per slot of hashing). This restores the literal CGY binding without changing any count in §11–§12.
3. **Message-level batching.** One honest message may carry the reveals of several slots and shares of other slots. The simulator knows all of these values before the message is produced, because a reveal is enabled only after all commitments of its slot. So the per-slot modifications of Lemmas I.1/I.2 can all be made first and then covered by one programmed proof.
4. **Adaptive statement selection.** A corrupted trustee chooses which enabled items it batches, and in what order. This is grinding over H₁ inputs and is covered by the Q factor of Theorem 8.2. It cannot change a statement's content: reveals are bound by earlier commitments, and u_j comes from the verifier's own state.
5. **Pre-weighting.** Lemma I.2 Lagrange-combines unweighted shares. Here W_i = h_i^{λ_i} for the fixed set of all a trustees. The membership statement w_i = u^{λ_i s_i} is equivalent, and the soundness step of Lemma I.2 is unchanged.

**Cumulative soundness bound for one public replay.** Let:

* Q₁, Q₃ = hash queries to the `Lin` seed and challenge oracles;
* Q_c = hash queries to the PoK-CSZ challenge oracle;
* B = number of messages (B = 810 at n = 1,024);
* each verifier draw fresh 128-bit RLC coefficients per message.

The challenge c = SHA-256 output mod q is not uniform, but every residue has probability ≤ ⌈2^{256}/q⌉/2^{256} < 2^{−250}; soundness uses only this maximum.

Pr[the replay accepts a transcript containing a false linear statement or a false CSZ statement]

≤ 2Q₁·2^{−128} (coefficient cancellation, enc and dec halves, Lemma 3.1 with grinding)
+ 2Q₃·2^{−250} (Lin challenge, max-probability bound)
+ Q_c·2^{−250} (PoK-CSZ Fiat–Shamir special soundness)
+ B·2^{−128} (outer RLC with fresh verifier coefficients, Lemma 3.1)
+ ε_PRG (verifier coefficient generator) + ε_sig (Ed25519) + Q²·2^{−257} (commitment collisions).

With Q₁, Q₃, Q_c ≤ 2^{64} and B ≤ 2^{10}, this is about **2^{−63}**, dominated by grinding against the 128-bit hashed coefficients.

* Doubling the hashed coefficient width to 256 bits reduces this term to Q·2^{−250}.
* That costs 2× on the coefficient-sized MSM windows; the verifier's fresh 128-bit RLC is unaffected.

**Recorded unresolved obligations:**

* **O1** (commit–reveal realizes F_SB in the observable ROM);
* **O2** (optional binding of X);
* the SUC composition of a single batched proof spanning several gate sessions. CGY prove each gate as an independent session with a distinct RO prefix ("different prefix per session"), whereas one `Lin` seed spans several sessions. The argument in item 3 covers this; a formal statement of multi-session batching within the SUC composition theorem (CGY Theorem H.3) is **O3**.

### 8.4 Joint decryption proof (second protocol)

**Protocol.**

1. Trustees exchange shares and compute C_j = Σ_i w_{i,j}.
2. β = H(ctx, (u_j, C_j)_j) and U* = Σβ_j u_j.
3. Each trustee hash-commits to (A_i, B_i) = ([r_i]g, [r_i]U*), then opens.
4. e = H(seed, ΣA_i, ΣB_i); z_i = r_i + eσ_i.
5. The aggregator checks each z_i (blame) and publishes (C_j)_j, A = ΣA_i, B = ΣB_i and z = Σz_i.

**Security.**

* **Soundness [standard].** Chaum–Pedersen for (g, h; U*, C*) with C* = Σβ_jC_j. Rewinding at e extracts x with honest witnesses known to the reduction. Coefficient soundness as in Theorem 8.2, so the error is about 2Q·2^{−128}.
* **ZK.** The hash-commit round prevents a rushing adversary from choosing its nonces after seeing honest ones, which a simulator needs to program e. This avoids ROS-type issues of 2-round variants **[standard, MuSig-1 style]**.
* **What is proved.** Correctness of the *combined* decryption. Errors that cancel across trustees do not change any C_j and are accepted by design; individual blame is the aggregator's job.

**Cost.** Four trustee rounds instead of one.

**Applicability.**

* **Koshelev / Divide-and-Pair (Tate)** applies exactly to BabyJubjub:
  * the 8-torsion is rational and cyclic;
  * 8 | p − 1, so μ₈ ⊂ F_p (embedding degree 1 for the 8-torsion);
  * the pairing is non-degenerate on E/8E ≅ Z/8.
* **Pornin halving** applies too: the torsion is cyclic, so the method needs 2 halvings, i.e. square roots, plus 3 Legendre symbols. In this field each square root costs more than the single exponentiation of the pairing test, so it was not implemented; the Tate test is the strongest feasible exact baseline. External numbers (7.5× on JubJub in Go, Divide-and-Pair) are on a different curve and library and are cited only as context.

**Findings.**

1. **Constant-size batching** (`rfc9497`, `lin`) cuts public proof bytes from 3 group elements per share to 3 per trustee. The shares themselves (32 B each) then dominate, giving 4.7 MB → 1.57 MB, a 3× saving vs `cp`.
2. **`lin` verifies 2.0–3.2× faster than the literal RFC 9497 structure** (in this sweep), and 3.2–4.4× faster than per-share commitment-form proofs in one MSM, for k ≥ 64. **The matched ablation (§13.1) attributes this to two standard optimizations:**
   * 128-bit coefficients (1.19×);
   * one batch-normalized statement hash instead of a per-composite hash with its own normalization (1.61×).

   With both, a challenge–response verifier (`rfc9497-opt`) is **as fast as `lin`**: 0.250 vs 0.259 s at k = 16,384. Commitment form itself is a 1.39× *slowdown*, because its MSM needs full-width coefficients (z·β, c·β) where the CR verifier uses 128-bit composites. One MSM across trustees (1.11×) and shared-base merging (1.20×) only recover that loss. The architectural benefit of commitment form is therefore *only* that it joins a larger message MSM, as in CGY where the CSZ proofs are in commitment form anyway.
3. **`joint`** publishes only the k combined decryptions plus one proof: 3× fewer public bytes than `lin`, and 4.3× faster in-memory public verification than RFC 9497 at k = 16,384 (2.0× end to end with public-input decoding, §13.2). The cost is **4 trustee rounds instead of 1**, and the 1.57 MB of shares move to the trustee-to-trustee channel. It suits deployments where public-record size matters (ElectionGuard's motivation) and trustee latency does not.
4. **Proving** is dominated by the t·k share multiplications. The batched methods are 1.8–3× cheaper than per-share proofs because they drop two multiplications per share.

## 14. Communication and computation trade-off

**Cost model.** T_total = T_prove + T_verify + T_decode + T_communication.

* T_prove, T_verify and T_decode are measured (§12).
* T_communication is modeled from the measured bytes.

**Critical path in the distributed tally.**

* The trustees' work per message is proving (sequential along the mask chain) plus verifying every other trustee's message.
* The board forwards each message to the other a − 1 trustees.
* Formats change bytes and per-message compute, but not the schedule or the number of rounds.

### 14.1 A quantitative format-selection rule (validated on held-out workloads)

**Rule.** Predict a format's public verification time from **exact operation counts** of its transcript, then choose

argmin over formats of T̂_verify + bytes / bandwidth (+ rounds × RTT when formats differ in rounds, as `joint` does).

**Counts used:**

* compressed and affine points decoded;
* points checked exactly (Tate) or with [q]P;
* MSM terms, scaled by 1/log₂ of the average MSM size per message (Pippenger);
* independent multiplications;
* items and messages.

**Unit costs.** Fitted by non-negative least squares on the **training** workloads only (n = 8, 64; 9 formats; 18 medians). The fitted values are physically plausible: compressed decode 7.2 µs vs 23.7 µs single-thread, and Tate 4.8 µs vs 11.9 µs, consistent with roughly 3× parallel speedup on 4 vCPUs.

* **CGY:** {V9, V5, V6} at every n ≥ 64. V1-Tate is on the frontier only at n = 8. V3, V4, V7, V8 and V1 are dominated.
* **Threshold decryption:**
  * `lin` and the optimized challenge–response verifier `rfc9497-opt` are on the frontier together (same bytes, equal time); both dominate the literal RFC 9497 structure and the per-share methods;
  * `joint` dominates on public bytes and verification, but costs 3 extra trustee rounds;

* public verifiers on ≥ 50 Mbit/s links should use **V6**;
* around 25 Mbit/s: **V5** (n ≤ 256) or **V9** (n = 1,024);
* ≤ 10 Mbit/s, archival storage or on-chain data availability, where bytes dominate: **V9**, whose verification also maps onto the GPU (§15).

* cofactor clearing.

**Classification: `VALUABLE_BUT_INCREMENTAL_EXTENSION`.**

**Strongest defensible novelty claim.**

> For a real threshold-MPC proof stream (CGY conditional gates) and for threshold ElGamal decryption, we classify the per-message statements (subspace / disjunction / group-element representation), implement a format family that preserves the exact proof relations, and show on a measured Pareto frontier with strong baselines (exact pairing-based membership; optimized batched DLEQ):
>
> * that batching the linear statements removes 25% of the CGY transcript and speeds replay 1.16–1.27× and tally up to 1.36×;
> * that a compact format (V9) is the only one to beat the strongest legacy verifier on both axes (−21% bytes, 1.2–1.4× faster);
> * that a count-based cost model selects the best format on held-out workloads.

That is a systems measurement built from established cryptography, applied to two protocols.

**Closest published work, per component:**

* batched DLEQ: Henry 2014; Davidson et al. PoPETs 2018; RFC 9497 §2.2; for threshold decryption, Aditya et al. ACNS 2004;
* batch ZK for homomorphisms: Peng–Boyd–Dawson; Gennaro et al.;
* exact membership: Koshelev 2022/037; Divide-and-Pair 2026/749;
* joint decryption proofs: ElectionGuard 2.x;
* cofactor handling: Decaf/Ristretto;
* the leak: Lim–Lee small-subgroup attacks.

No single paper combines these for conditional-gate transcripts, but each improvement is attributable to one of them.

**Practical performance advantage** [measured CPU, n = 1,024, one paired run; n = 256 three runs]:

* **CGY:**
  * V5 vs V3: −25% bytes, replay 1.16×, tally 1.36×, proving −25%;
  * V6 vs V4: −24% bytes, replay 1.19×;
  * V9 vs V1-Tate: −21% bytes, replay 1.41×;
  * fastest public replay overall: V6 at 14.2 s, vs 48.2 s for V1-Tate and 66.3 s for V1.
* **Threshold decryption at k = 16,384:**
  * against the literal RFC 9497 structure, batched verification is 1.85× faster;
  * against an RFC 9497-style verifier with the same two standard optimizations (128-bit composites, one batched statement hash), it is **equal** (0.250 vs 0.259 s);
  * with public-input decoding and exact validation included, differences shrink to 1.19× (`lin` vs RFC 9497);
  * `joint` halves public verification time (0.58 vs 1.15 s) and cuts public bytes 3×, for 3 extra trustee rounds.

**Remaining security obligations.**

* O1: commit–reveal realizes F_SB in the observable and programmable ROM;
* O2: optionally bind the slot ciphertexts X in the `Lin` seed;
* O3: a formal multi-session batching statement within CGY's SUC composition theorem;
* the hashed coefficients' grinding term 2Q·2^{−128} (use 256-bit coefficients if 2^{−128}-per-query security is required);
* the security of half-aggregation (C4) is open if pursued.

**Assessment against the decision gate.**

* **Strong candidate (precise new theorem or broadly applicable method): no.** No new theorem survived the audit, and the threshold-decryption speedup reduced, under controlled ablation, to standard optimizations.
* **Moderate candidate (valuable combination, substantial benefits in two protocols, clear systems contribution): partially.** The benefits are real but modest (1.16–1.41× and −21–25% bytes in CGY). In threshold decryption they reduce to a well-optimized RFC 9497 verifier, plus the known joint-proof trade-off. The validated selection rule is the most general piece, but it is a 7-feature regression.
* **Limited candidate:** the results are better than this — they change engineering conclusions (Tate baseline; V6/V9 Pareto) and come with a resolved composition argument.

**Venue suitability.**

* Not an applied-cryptography venue on its own: the cryptography is established.
* At most a workshop or short systems paper. It is better as the **implementation and evaluation section of the main winner-only voting paper** (BlockSys line), where V5/V6/V9 and the Tate baseline directly improve the verifier and transcript numbers.

**Is further investment in this line justified? No, not in its current form.**

1. **Conclude the cofactor/representation and GPU-MSM line.**
   * Adopt V6, or V5 where bytes matter, as the verifier-facing format and V9 for archival or low-bandwidth publication.
   * Report the Tate-based V1 as the baseline.
   * Fold the result into the main paper.
2. **Pivot (specific):** the only remaining lever that could improve **both** bytes and verification substantially is the **disjunctive masking proof** (PoK-CSZ). It is 61–74% of transcript bytes and 20 of 26 verifier terms per (gate, trustee). Research question:

   > Can the k sign-masking proofs of one trustee message be replaced by a single O(log k)-size argument that k ciphertext pairs were multiplied by secret signs s_j ∈ {±1} and rerandomized, sound against adversarially related ciphertext bases?

   Candidate approaches:
   * commit-and-prove with compressed Σ-protocols (Attema–Cramer–Fehr), committing to the sign vector under independent generators;
   * a Bulletproofs-style binary argument;
   * comparison with shuffle arguments, which prove a related permutation-plus-rerandomization relation.

   Closest work: ACF 2020/2021; Bayer–Groth shuffles; Henry–Goldberg batch OR proofs (and the Peng–Bao break, which a design must avoid). A positive answer would be a cryptographic contribution with a measurable end-to-end effect: at n = 1,024 it could remove up to ~70 MB of the 95 MB V5 transcript **[projection]**. A pre-study should first bound prover cost and verifier MSM size analytically before any implementation.
3. **Local GPU task (short, decision-oriented).** Run `reproduce_t009.ps1 -Gpu` on the RTX 4050:
   * measure V9-GPU against V6 (§15);
   * if V9-GPU does not approach V6, GPU acceleration should be dropped from the research narrative.

**Measurement boundaries.**
