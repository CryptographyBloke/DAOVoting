### 2.3 Remaining research gap

No **cryptographic** gap survived the audit:

* every primitive used in T007–T009 is established;
* T008's leak is a known attack class;
* the strongest new *construction-level* idea, batched DLEQ, is standardized.

The gap that remains is **systems-level and quantitative**. No published work measures the following for real threshold-MPC proof streams on a cofactor curve:

* how much of the transcript is linear (batchable to constant size) versus disjunctive (not batchable without compressed-Σ machinery);
* what exact membership costs once a pairing-based test is used;
* where the communication–computation Pareto frontier lies across representation (native/lifted, compressed/affine) and proof form (challenge–response, commitment, batched).

T009 therefore invested in a format family that spans this space, and measured it (§11–§14).


## 3. Corrections to earlier security and measurement statements

The T005–T008 reports are left byte-for-byte unchanged; the corrections are recorded here.

### 3.1 The T008 leak is a known attack class

T008 §1–§2 presents the torsion-dependent sign leak as a finding. Mechanically, it is the **small-subgroup confinement attack** (Lim–Lee 1997):

* an honest party applies a secret-dependent operation s·(·) to an attacker-supplied element whose component in a small subgroup survives;
* the output reveals s modulo that subgroup's exponent.

Here s ∈ {±1}, the subgroup is E[8](F_p), and an order-8 tag distinguishes +T from −T.

* **What remains a valid T008 contribution:**
  * the observation that T007's "free lift" bookkeeping re-introduced this classical channel into an MPC masking chain;
  * the regression test;
  * the repair.
* **What is not novel:** it is not a new attack mechanism, and a paper must cite the small-subgroup literature.

### 3.2 Error bound of the aggregate checks

T008 §4 states: *"For a batch containing at most 2^20 terms, the implemented aggregate check has ideal-fresh-uniform-coefficient false acceptance at most 1/q, bounded by 2^{−128} for the selected coefficient width."*

That is not the right bound for the implemented coefficients. All T007–T009 aggregate checks (`sigma::Acc::add`, `batch::Certified::check`) draw

ρ = (u₁ ‖ u₂) with u₁, u₂ independent 64-bit words, i.e. ρ uniform on S = [0, 2^{128}), embedded in Z_q.

**Lemma 3.1 [standard].** Let R₁,…,R_m ∈ G (|G| = q prime, q > 2^{128}) with some R_k ≠ O, and let ρ₁,…,ρ_m be independent and uniform on S. Then

Pr[Σ_j ρ_j R_j = O] ≤ 1/|S| = 2^{−128},

independent of m.

*Proof.* Condition on all ρ_j, j ≠ k. The equation becomes ρ_k R_k = −Σ_{j≠k} ρ_j R_j. Since R_k has order q, at most one residue of ρ_k mod q satisfies it. The elements of S are distinct mod q because 2^{128} < q. ∎

Hence:

* the bound is **2^{−128}, not 1/q** (1/q ≈ 2^{−251} would need coefficients uniform on Z_q);
* the "at most 2^{20} terms" condition is unnecessary;
* for B independent checks the bound is B·2^{−128}, and with an OS-seeded PRG one adds its distinguishing advantage. T008 §12 already states this correctly.

Two further conditions apply:

* **Fiat–Shamir-derived coefficients** (T007 `--v2-coeff fs`, and the coefficients α_k, β_j inside the T009 batched proof): the adversary can grind statements, giving Q_H·2^{−128} in the random-oracle model, where Q_H is the number of hash queries (Theorem 8.2).
* **Membership is a precondition** (Boyd–Pavlovski). The lemma needs every R_j ∈ G; T007 Proposition 2 shows a torsion residual survives with probability 1/2.

## 4. Secure representation framework

### 4.1 Definitions

* **Groups.** E = E(F_p) is the rational group of BabyJubjub (arkworks a = 1 twisted Edwards form, p the BN254 scalar field). Then

  #E = h·q with h = 8 and q prime (251 bits), and E ≅ K × G,

  where:
  * **G** is the prime-order subgroup;
  * **K** = E[8](F_p) is the kernel of [h] on E. It is cyclic of order 8 (checked by `group::torsion8`).
* **Projection.** π(L) = [h]L is a surjective homomorphism E → G with kernel K, because [8](K × G) = 0 × G. Its fibres π^{−1}(P) = L₀ + K have exactly 8 elements.
* **Canonical representative.** c(P) = [h^{−1} mod q]P is the unique fibre element lying in G.
* **Uniform fibre representative.** R(P) = c(P) + U with U ← K uniform. Equivalently, R(P) = L + U for any L ∈ π^{−1}(P), since K is a group.
* **Encodings** used by the formats:

  | Encoding | Wire value | Receiver |
  |---|---|---|
  | lifted (v2–v6) | any L with π(L) = P | uses π(L) |
  | canonical (T008 reference) | c(P) | uses π |
  | randomized (v3–v6) | R(P) | uses π |
  | native (v1, v7–v9) | P | checks P ∈ G exactly |

* **Abstract and wire transcripts.**
  * The *abstract protocol* Π is CGY over G: every message element is in G, and honest computations depend only on group values.
  * The *wire protocol* Π_enc sends encodings. A receiver's semantics are a function of π(·) of every received element.

### 4.2 Representation-security definition

Let A be a static adversary corrupting a set of trustees. It may choose, adaptively within the run, the wire representations of all elements it sends.

> **Definition 4.1 (representation transcript simulatability).** Π_enc is *representation-safe* relative to Π if there is an efficient simulator S such that, for every A, the joint view of A in Π_enc is identically distributed (or computationally indistinguishable) to S(view of A in Π). Here S sees only the abstract (canonical) transcript and A's own messages.

When this holds, every security property of Π against static adversaries — privacy (CGY Theorem 1 class), correctness and verifiability — transfers to Π_enc with the same advantage plus the distinguishing gap. This holds provided the abstract-level proofs are equivalent, which T007 Theorem 4 establishes for the commitment-form proofs.

### 4.3 Conditions and theorem

An implementation is representation-safe if it meets six conditions.

* **(C1) Canonical semantics.** Every honest predicate and every honest output value is a function of canonical values only. This covers acceptance, abort, identity checks, Fiat–Shamir challenges, batch coefficients, commitment hashes and opened values. Raw bytes may enter only the authentication of the sender's own message.
* **(C2) Fresh pads.** Every honest outgoing element is sent as L + U, where:
  * U ← K is uniform and independent across elements, messages and actions;
  * U is independent of all secrets and of all incoming representations;
  * L is any fibre element computed by honest arithmetic.

  Alternatively, an element is sent as a deterministic function of its canonical value (c(P), or the fibre minimum of §4.5).
* **(C3) Separate randomness.** Pad randomness never reuses witness or proof-nonce randomness.
* **(C4) Version separation.** Signatures, transcript headers and Fiat–Shamir domains include the format version, so an encoding cannot be reinterpreted under another format's semantics.
* **(C5) Representation-independent timing.** Honest computation time does not depend on the torsion components of incoming representations. This is a host-side assumption. arkworks' complete Edwards formulas are not data-dependent in the point, but variable-time scalar multiplication depends on the scalar.
* **(C6) Abort semantics.** The decision to abort depends only on canonical values. With randomized and lifted formats a torsion-only change never aborts; with native formats a torsion component always aborts. Either way the behaviour is determined by A's own message.

> **Theorem 4.2 [new as stated; elementary].** Under (C1)–(C6), the lifted-randomized formats (v3, v4, v5, v6) and the native formats (v7, v8, v9) are representation-safe relative to their abstract protocols, *perfectly* for ideal pads.

*Proof sketch.*

1. **Native formats.** Every accepted element is in G and is the abstract element itself, so S is the identity.
2. **Randomized formats, one element.** Consider an honest outgoing element L + U. Its canonical part P = π(L) is determined by the abstract execution. Its torsion part (L − c(P)) + U is uniform on K and independent of L, because U is a one-time pad in the group K, and by (C2) also independent of every other pad. So S outputs c(P) + U' with fresh U'.
3. **A's influence.** By (C1) and (C6), A's choices of incoming representations affect honest behaviour only through canonical values, which S sees in the abstract run, and through accept/abort events that are functions of A's own message.
4. **Many elements.** Repeated, correlated and multi-coordinate elements, and adaptively chosen messages within a static corruption, are covered because the pads are independent per coordinate (C2) and nothing else depends on representations (C1).
5. **Realised pads.** With a ChaCha20 pad stream the statement holds up to the PRG advantage. ∎

**Scope.**

* **Static corruption only.** Adaptive corruption reveals pad state. That state is independent of secrets, so it adds nothing beyond the corrupted party's secrets, but adaptive security of CGY itself is a separate question that this argument does not settle.
* **What follows from the representation argument:** T008's leak is closed; malleable wire bytes do not affect semantics.
* **What still depends on the CGY proof:** privacy, verifiability and correctness of the abstract protocol, and the soundness of the proof systems (T007 Theorem 4; §8 here).
* **Implementation status:**
  * the T008 code meets (C1)–(C4);
  * T009 extends it to the new item types: the `Lin5` commitments are padded from a separately seeded stream, domain `wod/v5/representation-torsion`;
  * T009 adds version-separated signature domains for v5–v9.

### 4.4 Exact membership instead of representations

The native formats replace representations by an exact check. For BabyJubjub, `subgroup::in_subgroup_tate` computes

t₈(T, P) = f_{8,T}(P)^{(p−1)/8}, with f_{8,T} = ℓ_T⁴ · ℓ_{2T}² / (v_{2T}⁴ · v_{4T}),

and accepts iff t₈ = 1.

**Why the test is exact.**

* T is a rational point of order 8, and 8 | p − 1.
* The reduced Tate pairing E[8](F_p) × E/8E → μ₈ is non-degenerate (Frey–Rück). With E/8E ≅ Z/8 cyclic, P ↦ t₈(T, P) has kernel exactly 8E = G.
* The lines are monic in Y or X, hence normalised at O, so no constant survives the exponentiation. Torsion points, the only points where the function can vanish or be undefined, are decided separately: a torsion point is in G iff it is O.

**Evaluation.** The function is evaluated projectively from Edwards coordinates through the Montgomery/Weierstrass map, with no inversion, followed by one exponentiation.

**Tests.** The test checks all 8 torsion classes, 64 random rational points against [q]P, and the character property t₈(P + kT) = t₈(P + T)^k.

**Cost.**

| Test | Cost per point [measured CPU, single thread] |
|---|---:|
| Tate test | 11.9 µs |
| [q]P with wNAF | 77.4 µs |
| Compressed decode | 23.7 µs |
| [8]-clear | 0.76 µs |

Pornin-style halving needs, for Sylow-2 ≅ Z/8, two square roots and three Legendre symbols. A square root in this field costs a Tonelli–Shanks exponentiation plus a 2-adicity-28 loop, so halving is not expected to beat the single-exponentiation pairing test here **[projection]**.

### 4.5 Deterministic coset representatives

* **Fibre minimum.** A deterministic, efficiently computable representative is the minimum of the 8 compressed encodings of L + K, i.e. canonical bytes for the quotient E/K. It costs:
  * for the sender: 7 additions + 8 normalizations (one batched inversion);
  * for a receiver checking canonicity: the same.

  It is simulatable without randomness and makes wire bytes non-malleable, as Ristretto does, but it is about 8× the sender cost of a pad.
* **c(P) = [8^{−1}]P.** Also deterministic but costs a scalar multiplication per element (T008 "canonical" policy).
* **Ristretto-style quotient encoding.** An encoding with a single square root and built-in canonicity would be the attractive option. Ristretto's formulas depend on p ≡ 5 mod 8 (via `SQRT_RATIO_M1`); BabyJubjub has p ≡ 1 mod 2²⁸. A BabyJubjub variant is **[open]**.

The **simplest defensible construction** is the native format with the exact Tate test:

* it has no representation layer at all (Theorem 4.2 is trivial for it);
* its cost over lifts is one exponentiation per received point (§11).

The randomized pad remains the cheapest secure *lifted* format.

## 7. Selected construction

**Research question.** For a threshold protocol whose public transcript is a stream of signed messages, each carrying many proofs, which per-message proof format minimises

T_total = T_prove + T_verify + T_decode + T_communication

on CPU and GPU hardware, subject to exactly preserving the protocol's proof relations? The relations are:

* membership for linear statements;
* knowledge for PoK-CSZ.

**Algorithm (trustee i, one message).**

1. **Produce items.**
   * Masks: commitment-form CSZ (v5, v6, v8) or challenge–response CSZ (v9).
   * Rerandomizer reveals and decryption shares: **without per-item proofs**.
2. **Batch proof.** Build `LinStmt` from the message's reveals (canonical R_k, labels) and shares (u_j from the state, canonical w_j, labels). Prove it with one `Lin` (§8.1) and append it as the last item.
3. **Encode points.**
   * v5/v6: lifts with fresh K-pads (compressed / affine);
   * v8/v9: native canonical points.
4. **Sign** under `wod/msg/v{version}`.

**Verifier (every receiver and the public verifier).**

1. Check the signature and the counter.
2. Decode the points:
   * v5/v6: π = [8] on every point;
   * v8/v9: exact Tate test on every point, reject on failure.
3. Run the state checks, including the reveal commitments `wod/v5/rr-commit`.
4. Verify:
   * v9: CSZ as one batch of independent variable-base multiplications (`MulBackend::run`: CPU or the T006 GPU kernel);
   * all other relations (commitment-form CSZ, `Lin`): one random linear combination with fresh verifier coefficients in [0, 2^{128}), evaluated as **one MSM per message**.
5. If the combination fails, re-check per proof to report the offending item.
6. Apply the items; finish gates with pre-weighted shares.

**Invariants (each with regression tests, §10).**

| Invariant | Statement |
|---|---|
| I1 | Every value used semantically is canonical and in G (π or the exact test) |
| I2 | A message has a `Lin5` iff it has linear statements; it is last and unique |
| I3 | The `Lin` seed binds the session, version, sender, W_i and the ordered labelled canonical statements |
| I4 | Batch coefficients are fresh verifier randomness (outer RLC) or hash outputs over the full statement (inner `Lin`), 128 bits |
| I5 | Format admission is exact: no per-item and batched items mixed; v1-form masks only in v1/v9; signature domains differ per version |
| I6 | Pre-weighted shares against W_i for the fixed decryption set |


## 8. Mathematical security arguments

### 8.1 Statement and proof (formats v5, v6, v8, v9)

Fix a trustee message from sender i with weighted key W = W_i = [σ]g (σ = λ_i s_i, public W).

**Statement.** Its canonical *linear statement*, in item order, is

S = (enc: (ℓ_k, R_k)_k, dec: (ℓ_j, u_j, w_j)_j),

where:

* R_k ∈ G² are the revealed rerandomizers;
* u_j is the first component of the opened ciphertext Y' of slot j (engine state, fixed by earlier messages);
* w_j is the pre-weighted share;
* ℓ are slot/trustee labels.

**Language.** Let x = dlog_g h. Then

L_lin = { S : ∀k R_k.b = [x]R_k.a  ∧  ∀j w_j = [σ]u_j }.

Membership is well defined without a witness (G is cyclic).

**Proof.** π = (T₁, T₂, T₃, T₄; z₁, z₂), with each half present iff its list is non-empty:

1. seed = H₁("wod/v5/lin-seed", ctx, version, i, g, W, S);
2. α_k, β_j = first 128 bits of H₂(seed, "enc"/"dec", index);
3. c = H₃(ctx, seed, T₁..T₄), full width.

**Verification** accepts iff

* [z₁]g = T₁ + c·Σα_k R_k.a;
* [z₁]h = T₂ + c·Σα_k R_k.b;
* [z₂]g = T₃ + c·W;
* [z₂]U* = T₄ + c·D*, where U* = Σβ_j u_j and D* = Σβ_j w_j.

These rows join the message's random linear combination. U* and D* are never formed: their terms go straight into the MSM.

**Honest prover.** κ* = Σα_k ρ_k; T₁ = [a]g, T₂ = [a]h, T₃ = [b]g, T₄ = [b]U*; z₁ = a + cκ*, z₂ = b + cσ.

### 8.2 Theorems

> **Theorem 8.1 (completeness) [standard].** Honest proofs verify.

> **Theorem 8.2 (soundness, ROM) [application of Henry / Davidson et al. / BGR].** For any adversary making at most Q queries to H₁ and H₃,
>
> Pr[accept ∧ S ∉ L_lin] ≤ 2Q·2^{−128} + 2Q/q.
>
> The bound covers adversarially chosen statements, reordering and adaptive grinding of S.

*Proof.* Define the errors ε_k = R_k.b − [x]R_k.a and δ_j = w_j − [σ]u_j, and their combinations ε* = Σα_kε_k and δ* = Σβ_jδ_j.

1. **Enc part.** From the two enc rows, ([z₁]h − T₂) − [x]([z₁]g − T₁) = c·ε*, i.e. −(T₂ − [x]T₁) = c·ε*. The left side is fixed before c, which is a fresh H₃ output.
2. **Dec part.** From the dec rows, with τ₃ = dlog_g T₃ (which forces z₂ = τ₃ + cσ): [τ₃]U* − T₄ = c·δ*, with the left side again fixed before c.
3. **Challenge step.** If ε* ≠ O (resp. δ* ≠ O), each H₃ query succeeds with probability ≤ 1/q.
4. **Coefficient step.** If some ε_k ≠ O but ε* = O: α is a deterministic function of the H₁ output on S, so for each H₁ query Lemma 3.1 bounds Pr[ε* = O] by 2^{−128}. The same holds for δ.
5. A union bound over queries gives the theorem. ∎

> **Theorem 8.3 (zero knowledge, ROM) [standard].** The proof is HVZK and, with H₃ programmed, NIZK. The simulator picks c, z₁, z₂ and solves for T₁..T₄.

**Proof versus knowledge.** π proves *membership* plus knowledge of σ and of the combination κ*, **not** of each ρ_k.

### 8.3 Composition with the CGY gate

* **Per item.** CGY uses ZKP0 ("zero-knowledge proof that A_k encrypts 0") and DLEQ (correct decryption) as membership proofs. Only PoK-CSZ is a proof of knowledge. With C1:
  * correctness of every gate output follows from L_lin membership: the rerandomizers do not change plaintexts and the shares combine to [x]u_j;
  * the CSZ proofs are unchanged.
* **Over a run.** Over all B ≤ 810 messages of an n = 1,024 run, the soundness error is ≤ 2Q·2^{−128} + 2Q/q + B·2^{−128}. The last term is the verifier's own per-message RLC, with fresh verifier coefficients.
* **[conditional] extraction.** If the CGY simulation extracts *corrupt rerandomizer randomness* (e.g. to equivocate the reveal commitment), C1 must be replaced by an amortized proof of knowledge (Cramer–Damgård 2009). The CGY PDF was not available in this session to settle this. The naming (ZKP vs PoK) and the simulator's needs suggest that membership suffices:
  * honest decryption shares are programmed from corrupt key shares, which are known under static corruption;
  * rerandomizer randomness is not needed.

  A paper must check this against the CGY proof.
* **Pre-weighting.** The statement uses W_i = [λ_i]h_i for a fixed decryption set (q_dec = a). Variable decryption sets need the set before shares are produced (T007 §17).
* **Identity and boundaries.**
  * An identity u_j forces w_j = O.
  * If every u_j = O then U* = O, and the dec rows accept iff D* = O.
  * Both cases are tested.
  * Cancelling errors inside one message (±Δ) are rejected with probability 1 − 2^{−128} (tested).
* **Binding.** The seed binds:
  * ctx (session: setup + sealed board, hence poll);
  * the format version and sender index;
  * W_i;
  * each statement's slot label and canonical value, in order.

  Consequently a proof cannot be moved to another message, slot, trustee, poll or format (tested). Signatures bind raw bytes under a per-version domain (§3.4).
* **Commit–reveal.** The reveal commitment of v5–v9 (`wod/v5/rr-commit`) binds the canonical rerandomizers and the salt. There is no per-item proof to bind; the batch proof is checked against the opened values.

### 8.4 Joint decryption proof (second protocol)

**Protocol.**

1. Trustees exchange shares and compute C_j = Σ_i w_{i,j}.
2. β = H(ctx, (u_j, C_j)_j) and U* = Σβ_j u_j.
3. Each trustee hash-commits to (A_i, B_i) = ([r_i]g, [r_i]U*), then opens.
4. e = H(seed, ΣA_i, ΣB_i); z_i = r_i + eσ_i.
5. The aggregator checks each z_i (blame) and publishes (C_j)_j, A = ΣA_i, B = ΣB_i and z = Σz_i.

**Security.**

* **Soundness [standard].** Chaum–Pedersen for (g, h; U*, C*) with C* = Σβ_jC_j. Rewinding at e extracts x with honest witnesses known to the reduction. Coefficient soundness as in Theorem 8.2, so the error is about 2Q·2^{−128}.
* **ZK.** The hash-commit round prevents a rushing adversary from choosing its nonces after seeing honest ones, which a simulator needs to program e. This avoids ROS-type issues of 2-round variants **[standard, MuSig-1 style]**.
* **What is proved.** Correctness of the *combined* decryption. Errors that cancel across trustees do not change any C_j and are accepted by design; individual blame is the aggregator's job.

**Cost.** Four trustee rounds instead of one.
